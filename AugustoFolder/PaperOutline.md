# Low-Cost WiFi CSI-Based Drone Detection Using ESP32 Microcontrollers: A Preliminary Experimental Study


---

## Abstract

The increasing prevalence of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) in both commercial and recreational settings has created a growing need for accessible, low-cost drone detection systems. This paper presents a preliminary experimental investigation into WiFi Channel State Information (CSI) as a sensing modality for drone detection using two ESP32 microcontrollers as a low-cost transmitter-receiver pair. Following the Espressif esp-csi open-source framework, we collected labeled CSI recordings across three outdoor test configurations: a 264 ft antenna range characterization, a 50 ft comparison between human walking and drone flight, and a 105 ft three-class experiment distinguishing a clean baseline, controlled Fresnel zone drone passage, and random drone flight. Our results demonstrate that drone presence produces measurable and directionally consistent perturbations in CSI amplitude and RSSI compared to a human-only baseline, with the two signatures differing in both direction and temporal structure. A fitted path loss exponent of n = 2.59 is reported for the outdoor environment, consistent with near-free-space propagation with minor atmospheric effects. We identify the current packet rate of approximately 10 packets per second as the primary limitation preventing Doppler-based propeller frequency analysis, and outline a clear path toward higher-rate collection and machine learning classification as future work.

## 1. Introduction

Unmanned aerial vehicles have become increasingly accessible to the general public, with consumer drones now available at low cost and capable of carrying cameras, sensors, and payloads. While drones offer significant benefits across industries including agriculture, infrastructure inspection, and emergency response, their misuse poses serious risks to public safety, privacy, and airspace security [1]. Incidents involving drones near airports, government facilities, and public events have driven demand for reliable, deployable detection systems that do not require expensive radar infrastructure [24].

Existing drone detection approaches span a wide range of modalities including radar [6], acoustic sensing, optical/visual detection, and radio frequency (RF) fingerprinting [1][5]. Each approach carries tradeoffs between cost, range, accuracy, and deployability. Radar systems offer excellent range and accuracy but are prohibitively expensive for widespread deployment. Acoustic and visual methods are sensitive to environmental conditions such as wind and lighting. RF fingerprinting approaches, which analyze the WiFi or RF traffic generated by the drone itself, have demonstrated high detection accuracy [1][2] but require the drone to be actively transmitting, making them ineffective against drones operating in silent or autonomous modes.

A fundamentally different approach exploits WiFi Channel State Information (CSI) — not the drone's own transmissions, but the perturbation the drone causes to an existing WiFi signal path between two fixed nodes. CSI describes how a transmitted WiFi signal propagates across each OFDM subcarrier from transmitter to receiver [18]. Any object that enters the signal path between the two nodes modifies the multipath propagation environment, producing measurable changes in the per-subcarrier amplitude and phase of the received signal. This device-free passive sensing approach requires no cooperation from the drone and is not dependent on the drone transmitting any signal.

CSI-based sensing has been extensively studied for human activity recognition (HAR), fall detection, gesture recognition, and indoor localization [8][18]. Its application to drone detection is more recent and remains an open research problem, particularly at low cost and with commodity hardware. The Espressif ESP32 microcontroller exposes raw CSI data through its WiFi driver API, making it one of the most accessible and affordable platforms for CSI-based sensing research, at a total hardware cost of approximately $20 [14][15].

This paper makes the following contributions:

- We implement and deploy a dual-ESP32 CSI sensing system by following the open-source espressif/esp-csi get-started framework, extending the provided Python data collection and parsing scripts to support our experimental analysis needs.
- We collect and analyze labeled CSI recordings comparing drone flight, human walking, and empty baseline conditions at 50 ft and 105 ft outdoor antenna separations.
- We report a fitted outdoor path loss exponent of n = 2.59 from a 264 ft range characterization walk, consistent with near-line-of-sight outdoor propagation.
- We identify the key hardware and signal processing limitations of low-cost ESP32 CSI for drone detection and provide a concrete roadmap for future improvement.
